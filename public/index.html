Spot what moves in the infrared sky

SPHEREx is a NASA space telescope that photographs the whole sky in 102 infrared colours, coming back to every spot about every six months. SkyBlink lines up those pictures of one small patch and plays them as a flip-book.

Each frame is one SPHEREx look at this patch of sky. Things that jump between frames are moving. Things that get brighter and fainter are variable.

          See infrared with your TV remote

          1. Tap Start camera (the picture stays on your device; nothing is recorded or sent).
          2. Point a TV remote at the camera and hold down any button.
          3. Look for a flickering light on the remote's tip. Your eyes can't see it, but the camera can.

          No flicker? Many phone cameras block infrared; the front (selfie) camera often doesn't. Some remotes use radio instead of infrared.

          your eyes 0.4–0.7 µm remote ~0.94 µm SPHEREx 0.75–5 µm

          That light is infrared, just past red. Most remotes flash at about 0.94 µm, which is one of the 102 colours SPHEREx photographs the sky in.
