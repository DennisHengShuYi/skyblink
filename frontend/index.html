Spot what moves in the infrared sky

SPHEREx is a NASA space telescope that photographs the whole sky in 102 infrared colours, coming back to every spot about every six months. SkyBlink lines up those pictures of one small patch and plays them as a flip-book.

Each frame is one SPHEREx look at this patch of sky. Things that jump between frames are moving. Things that get brighter and fainter are variable.

Each frame is one SPHEREx look at this patch of sky. Things that jump between frames are moving. The red crosshair marks the position you asked for; north is up and east is left.

In Every image mode each picture is taken at a slightly different infrared wavelength (SPHEREx's filter changes across the detector). A red or blue star can brighten and fade just because the wavelength changed. Check the µm in the frame label, or sort by wavelength.

    B minus A. A moving star shows up as a blue/red pair: blue where it was, red where it went. Caution: bright and saturated stars can leave a pair even when they don't move, because the telescope's blur differs between images.
